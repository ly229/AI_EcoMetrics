# The Skill Content of Recent Technological Change

Author: David H. Autor, Frank Levy, and Richard J. Murnane
Year: 2003
Source: [MIT Economics profile](https://economics.mit.edu/people/faculty/david-h-autor)

## Key Idea
The paper argues that computerization changes the task content of jobs, substituting for routine cognitive work while complementing abstract and interpersonal tasks. This helps explain changing wage patterns and employment structure.

## Data
The paper uses U.S. occupational and task data to study how technological change altered the relative demand for different kinds of labor.

## Model / Method
The paper develops a task-based framework in which technology substitutes for some tasks and complements others, producing changes in occupational demand and wages.

## Main Takeaway
Routine, codifiable tasks are the most exposed to computerization. Abstract and interpersonal tasks become relatively more valuable, so technology shifts labor demand across the task spectrum rather than simply reducing employment everywhere.

## Why It Matters For This Study
It is foundational for understanding how automation and AI alter task demand rather than simply eliminating whole occupations.
