# From Clerks to Agentic AI: How Will Technology Transform the Labor Market in Finance?

## I. How Does Agentic AI Impact Financial Markets Through Automation?
- Motivation: finance has gone through distinct technology waves that changed how work is organized.
- Research question: how has task automation intensity affected labor productivity in U.S. financial firms?
- Why the setting matters: finance is information-intensive, scalable, and exposed to automation.
- Main contribution:
  - construct firm-level automation measures from filings,
  - relate them to productivity outcomes,
  - compare patterns across computerization, indexing, and AI eras.
- Preview of results:
  - productivity rises over time,
  - labor-cost adjustment is slower than output growth,
  - AI-era firms show the strongest automation intensity.

## II. Historical Lessons from the Computerization and Indexing Eras
- Define the three eras:
  - `Computerization` `1985-2000`
  - `Index investing` `2000-2015`
  - `AI / automation` `2015-present`
- Explain why these periods are economically meaningful.
- Describe the main technology shifts in each era:
  - computerization: terminals, spreadsheets, electronic trading, back-office automation
  - indexing: ETFs, passive replication, rules-based allocation
  - AI: machine learning, generative AI, automation tools, workflow augmentation
- Clarify that the eras are descriptive and interpretive, not a stand-alone identification strategy.

## III. How Does Automation Raise Labor Productivity?
- Automation can raise labor productivity by:
  - reducing routine manual work,
  - lowering coordination costs,
  - increasing workflow speed,
  - enabling greater scale with the same labor input.
- Technology can also change labor composition without immediate headcount reduction.
- Core conceptual variable:
  - `task_automation_intensity`
- Interpretation:
  - the proxy captures the extent to which routine, codifiable, or repetitive work is shifted from human labor to software, rules, machines, or AI systems.
- Testable implications:
  - productivity should rise more in later eras,
  - automation intensity should be associated with higher output per worker,
  - labor expense per worker may move more slowly than output measures.

## IV. How Do We Define Productivity and Automation Intensity?
### A. Who Do We Study?
- Publicly listed U.S. financial firms observed in the filing and accounting data.
- Main sample: 100 firms for the core analysis.
- Possible extension: 300 firms for robustness or appendix analysis.
- Use calendar-year cutoffs to assign firm-years to the computerization, indexing, and AI eras.
- State the inclusion and exclusion rules clearly so the sample remains transparent and reproducible.

### B. What Is the Productivity Measure?
- Primary outcome:
  - revenue per employee
- Secondary outcomes:
  - operating income per employee
  - labor expense per employee
  - operating expense / revenue
  - optional: AUM per employee for asset managers only

### C. How Is Task Automation Intensity Measured Across Eras?
- Build era-specific measures from annual reports and 10-K filings.
- `computerization_intensity`:
  - computers, terminals, spreadsheets, electronic trading, workflow systems, digitization, back-office automation
- `indexing_intensity`:
  - index funds, ETFs, passive management, systematic rebalancing, benchmark replication, rules-based allocation
- `ai_intensity`:
  - artificial intelligence, machine learning, generative AI, autonomous agents, robotic process automation, workflow automation, large language models
- Normalize each score by filing length.
- Optionally standardize within sample for comparability.
- If useful, combine the era-specific scores into one broad `task_automation_intensity` index.
