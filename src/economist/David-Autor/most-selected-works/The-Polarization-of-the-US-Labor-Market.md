# The Polarization of the U.S. Labor Market

Author: David H. Autor, Lawrence F. Katz, and Melissa S. Kearney
Year: 2006
Source: [MIT Economics profile](https://economics.mit.edu/people/faculty/david-h-autor)

## Key Idea
The paper documents labor-market polarization: employment growth is concentrated in high-skill and low-skill jobs while middling routine jobs shrink. Technology is a major driver because routine tasks are easier to automate.

## Data
The paper uses U.S. labor-market and occupational data covering several decades to show changes in employment shares across job categories.

## Model / Method
The analysis is empirical and descriptive, linked to a task-based interpretation of technological change and occupational restructuring.

## Main Takeaway
Middle-skill routine jobs are squeezed from both directions: high-skill analytical work expands at the top, while low-skill service work persists at the bottom. That pattern is one of the clearest empirical signatures of automation-driven restructuring.

## Why It Matters For This Study
It provides a clear empirical baseline for understanding why AI and automation may hollow out middle-skill work.
