# The Productivity Paradox of Information Technology

Author: Erik Brynjolfsson
Year: 1993
Source: [Communications of the ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/163263.163278)

## Key Idea
The paper argues that early computerization did not show up cleanly in aggregate productivity statistics, creating the "productivity paradox" of information technology. The core point is not that computers had no value, but that measured productivity gains lagged because benefits were often indirect, organizational, and complementary to changes in management and work design.

## Data
This work is largely a synthesis of then-current evidence on productivity measurement and information technology adoption rather than a single new dataset. It reviews firm- and industry-level patterns showing weak short-run aggregate productivity responses to IT investment.

## Model / Method
The paper is primarily conceptual and measurement-oriented. It emphasizes complementarity between IT, organizational capital, and workflow change, and argues that conventional output measures can miss quality and unpriced gains.

## Why It Matters For This Study
It sets up the core question behind later AI research: why transformative technology can be economically important even before standard productivity statistics fully capture its value.
