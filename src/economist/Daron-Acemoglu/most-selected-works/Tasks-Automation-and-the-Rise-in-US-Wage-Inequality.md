# Tasks, Automation, and the Rise in US Wage Inequality

Author: Daron Acemoglu and Pascual Restrepo
Year: 2022
Source: [MIT Economics working paper PDF](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/publications/Tasks%20Automation%20and%20the%20Rise%20in%20US%20Wage%20Inequalit.pdf)

## Key Idea
The paper argues that automation has played a major role in the rise of U.S. wage inequality by reducing the wages of workers specialized in routine tasks in industries with rapid automation. The authors show that task displacement is a central channel linking technology to distributional change.

## Data
The paper uses U.S. labor-market and industry data over roughly the last four decades, covering changes in wage structure from 1980 to 2016 and measures of automation exposure across industries and worker groups.

## Model / Method
The paper develops a task-based comparative-advantage framework in which automation expands the set of tasks that capital can perform. It then links wage changes for demographic groups to their exposure to task displacement.

## Main Takeaway
The paper’s core result is that automation is strongly associated with rising wage inequality when it displaces workers concentrated in routine tasks. The mechanism is task reallocation, not a uniform decline in the value of labor.

## Why It Matters For This Study
This is one of the most directly relevant papers for understanding how automation changes wages, inequality, and labor demand.
