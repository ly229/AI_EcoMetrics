Use this as the main title:

  AI Exposure and Labor Productivity in U.S. Financial Firms Across Three Technology Eras

  If you want something a bit sharper and more working-paper-like, I would also consider:

  - Technology Waves and Labor Productivity in U.S. Financial Firms
  - From Computerization to AI: Labor Productivity in U.S. Financial Firms
  - AI, Indexing, and Labor Productivity in Finance

  My recommendation is the first one. It keeps:

  - the AI focus,
  - the labor-productivity focus,
  - the finance setting,
  - and your era classification.

  ## Structured Outline

  ### 1. Introduction

  - Motivation: finance has gone through distinct technology waves.
  - Research question: how has AI exposure affected labor productivity in financial firms?
  - Why the setting matters: finance is information-intensive, scalable, and exposed to automation.
  - Main contribution:
      - construct a firm-level AI exposure measure from filings,
      - relate it to productivity outcomes,
      - compare patterns across computerization, indexing, and AI eras.
  - Preview of results.

  ### 2. Institutional Background and Era Classification

  - Define the three eras:
      - Computerization 1985–2000
      - Index investing 2000–2015
      - AI / automation 2015–present
  - Explain why these periods are economically meaningful.
  - Describe the main technology shifts in each era.
  - Clarify that the eras are descriptive and interpretive, not a stand-alone identification strategy.

  ### 3. Conceptual Framework

  - Technology can raise labor productivity through:
      - task automation,
      - scale economies,
      - workflow consolidation,
      - lower coordination costs.
  - Technology can also change labor composition without reducing labor expense immediately.
  - State testable implications:
      - productivity should rise more in later eras,
      - AI exposure should be associated with higher output per worker,
      - labor expense per worker may move more slowly than output measures.

  ### 4. Data

  #### 4.1 Sample

  - Publicly listed U.S. financial firms.
  - State the sample size target, e.g. 100 firms.
  - Explain inclusion and exclusion rules.

  #### 4.2 Firm Outcomes

  - Primary outcome:
      - revenue per employee
  - Secondary outcomes:
      - operating income per employee
      - labor expense per employee
      - operating expense / revenue
      - optional: AUM per employee for asset managers only

  #### 4.3 AI Exposure Measure

  - Construct from 10-K filings.
  - Count terms such as:
      - artificial intelligence
      - machine learning
      - generative AI
      - automation
  - Normalize by filing length.
  - Explain weighting if you use one.

  #### 4.4 Era Assignment

  - Assign firm-years to the three eras.
  - Clarify whether era is based only on calendar year or on firm-year exposure.

  ### 5. Empirical Strategy

  - Baseline descriptive comparisons across eras.
  - Firm and year fixed effects specification.
  - Main regression:
      - productivity outcome on AI exposure, era indicators, and fixed effects.
  - Optional interactions:
      - AI exposure × era
      - passive-heavy firms × era
      - firm type × era
  - Explain this is primarily a descriptive panel design, not a causal identification strategy.

  ### 6. Results

  #### 6.1 Long-Run Productivity Trends by Era

  - Plot productivity over time.
  - Show how averages differ across the three eras.
  - Compare trends across firm types.

  #### 6.2 AI Exposure and Productivity

  - Main regression table.
  - Discuss whether higher AI exposure is linked to higher productivity.

  #### 6.3 Labor Cost Response

  - Show whether labor expense per employee changes alongside productivity.
  - Interpret whether the evidence points to substitution, augmentation, or reorganization.

  #### 6.4 Heterogeneity

  - Compare:
      - asset managers vs banks,
      - passive-heavy vs active-heavy firms,
      - large vs mid-sized firms.

  ### 7. Discussion

  - What the results imply about the role of technology in finance.
  - Why productivity gains may show up before labor-cost reductions.
  - Why indexing and AI may both matter, but in different ways.
  - How the findings fit the broader literature on automation and task reallocation.

  ### 8. Limitations

  - Filing language captures disclosure as well as adoption.
  - Public firms only.
  - Productivity ratios are influenced by business mix and market conditions.
  - Era classification is stylized and should be interpreted as a historical framework.

  ### 9. Conclusion

  - Restate the main finding.
  - Emphasize the three-era perspective.
  - State the broader implication:
      - finance becomes more scalable and more productive,
      - but labor adjustment is uneven and gradual.

  ## Best version of the paper’s core message

  You want the paper to say:

  Technology waves in finance have changed the amount of labor required per unit of output, and AI appears to be the latest phase of that long-run
  productivity shift.
