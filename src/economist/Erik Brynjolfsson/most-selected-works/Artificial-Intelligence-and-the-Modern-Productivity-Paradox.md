# Artificial Intelligence and the Modern Productivity Paradox

Author: Erik Brynjolfsson, Daniel Rock, and Chad Syverson
Year: 2017
Source: [NBER Working Paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24001)

## Key Idea
The paper revisits the productivity paradox in the age of AI. It argues that measured productivity may lag because general-purpose technologies require time, complementary investments, and reorganization before their benefits fully appear.

## Data
This is a theoretical and empirical synthesis paper. It discusses productivity trends, AI diffusion, and prior evidence on firm-level and aggregate productivity, but does not introduce one central dataset.

## Model / Method
The paper uses a general-purpose technology framework. AI is treated as a GPT that needs complementary innovations in organization, data, and capital before it delivers visible productivity effects.

## Main Takeaway
The paper suggests that AI’s aggregate impact may look modest at first because diffusion, complementarities, and reorganization take time. In the long run, however, the technology can still be transformative once firms learn how to use it well.

## Why It Matters For This Study
This is useful for understanding why AI may transform labor markets before the macro productivity statistics catch up.
