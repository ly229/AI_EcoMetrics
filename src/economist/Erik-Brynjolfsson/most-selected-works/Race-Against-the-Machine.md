# Race Against the Machine

Author: Erik Brynjolfsson and Andrew McAfee
Year: 2011
Source: [MIT Press Bookstore](https://mitpressbookstore.mit.edu/book/9780984725113)

## Key Idea
The book argues that digital technologies were accelerating innovation and productivity while simultaneously substituting for many routine human tasks. Its central warning is that technological progress can be strongly productivity-enhancing yet still depress employment and wages for large parts of the workforce.

## Data
This is a book-length synthesis drawing on evidence from labor economics, computerization, and firm performance. It relies on patterns in automation, digitization, and employment trends rather than a single empirical dataset.

## Model / Method
The framework is conceptual and economic rather than formal. It combines task substitution, skill-biased technical change, and the idea that automation can outpace the creation of new tasks for displaced workers in the short run.

## Main Takeaway
Automation can increase output and lower the cost of many services while still creating displacement pressure for routine workers. The paper’s practical message is that labor-market institutions and retraining determine whether the gains are broadly shared.

## Why It Matters For This Study
It is one of the clearest early statements that machine progress can reshape labor demand in ways that leave workers behind unless institutions and skills adjust.
