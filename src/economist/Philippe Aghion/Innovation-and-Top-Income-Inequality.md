# Innovation and Top Income Inequality

Author: Philippe Aghion, Ufuk Akcigit, Antonin Bergeaud, Richard Blundell, and David Hémous
Year: 2015
Source: [NBER Working Paper 21247](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21247)

## Key Idea
The paper argues that innovation is associated with higher top income inequality, but also with greater upward mobility. The main Schumpeterian message is that innovation raises top incomes because successful innovators earn rents, yet it also creates turnover and new opportunities that can increase mobility. The paper also finds that the top-income effect is weaker where lobbying intensity is higher.

## Data
The paper uses:
- U.S. cross-state panel data
- cross-U.S. commuting-zone data
- measures of innovation such as patents per capita and other innovativeness indicators
- measures of top income shares, especially the top 1% income share
- social mobility measures at the commuting-zone level
- state-level lobbying intensity

The sample period highlighted in the paper is 1975 to 2010 for the top-income-share analysis.

## Model / Method
The paper is empirically identified rather than built around a single structural model. It uses:
- cross-state panel regressions
- two distinct instrumentation strategies to address causality
- cross-section regressions at the commuting-zone level

The conceptual model is Schumpeterian: innovation creates rents, destroys incumbent positions, and reallocates economic rewards toward successful entrants and innovators.

## Why It Matters For This Study
This paper is directly relevant because it connects innovation and technological change to inequality at the top of the income distribution. That helps frame how AI, automation, and agentic systems may increase rewards to high-skill innovators while also reshaping mobility and labor-market structure.
