# How Will AI Trading Agents Transform Financial Markets?

After talking with old friends overseas and in Hong Kong, many of them former hedge fund colleagues, a common question kept coming up: in the age of artificial intelligence, especially in FICC trading, are we entering a new phase of transformation in financial markets?

This is really part of a broader pattern: how productivity-enhancing technologies reshape industries and the structure of markets themselves. In this case, the question is what AI will change in finance.

Since late last year, I have been working on my second book, *Witnessing Interconnectedness*, which is expected to be published later this year. The first chapter revisits the rise of FICC, especially how information technology, including semiconductors and computers in the 1970s and 1980s, reshaped financial market structure. These technologies made cross-asset connections easier and helped create entirely new investment strategies.

When we talk about AI today, it is natural to compare it with the earlier computing revolution. The real question is how AI-driven productivity will reshape financial markets, including the tools people use, the strategies they pursue, the structure of firms, and the role of market participants.

---

## The Era of FICC + Crypto

Over the past five to six years, traditional FICC assets have become increasingly integrated with crypto. This has strengthened correlations and made crypto an important indicator of market liquidity. We are now effectively in the FICC + C era.

Today, anyone trying to understand global liquidity needs to pay attention to high-valuation, highly liquid crypto assets such as Bitcoin.

That said, this article is not mainly about asset classes. It is about AI trading agents and how they may reshape investor behavior, especially for small institutions and younger professionals.

---

## Historical Parallel: The Computer Revolution

In the 1980s, technologies such as personal computers, spreadsheets, Bloomberg terminals, and Reuters systems expanded computational and analytical capacity. They enabled:

- Cross-asset modeling
- Correlation analysis across rates, FX, and commodities
- Macro hedge strategies and arbitrage

Markets changed in three important ways:

1. Speed increased from minutes to seconds, which helped launch programmatic trading.
2. Information processing improved dramatically, which accelerated the growth of derivatives.
3. Institutional advantage widened as hardware and algorithms became more important than manual execution.

Manual trading methods such as paper orders, phone calls, and floor shouting were gradually replaced by system-driven trading. Markets moved from fragmented and manual to systematic and interconnected.

By 2000:

- Institutional ownership had exceeded 70%
- Direct retail participation had fallen below 30%
- Markets had become more efficient and less emotional

This did not mean retail investors disappeared. It meant the market structure shifted toward professionalization.

## AI Competition: Rapid Stratification

Today looks a lot like the 1970s and 1980s.

- Productivity is rising
- Market structure is reorganizing
- Institutions are evolving

AI's core application in finance is AI trading agents, and their use is spreading quickly.

If other market participants adopt AI and you do not, your relative efficiency will fall.

### Retail Investor Divergence

Retail investors are increasingly splitting into two groups:

1. AI-enabled investors
   - Use AI for research, modeling, and execution
   - Are quickly approaching the capability level of small institutions

2. Non-AI users
   - Rely on social media and intuition
   - Are falling behind rapidly

AI trading agents are following the same path as programmatic trading, but at much greater speed and scale.

Key facts:

- AI now accounts for over 90% of global trading volume
- Algorithmic trading exceeds 70% in U.S. equities

---

## Democratization of Financial Capability

Open-source AI trading tools are spreading quickly.

Retail investors can now:

- Conduct deep research using natural language
- Monitor multiple markets in real time
- Automate risk management
- Execute trades end to end

This meaningfully reduces:

- Cost
- Complexity
- Information asymmetry

In the past, tools like Bloomberg or Reuters could cost tens of thousands of dollars per year. Today, AI can provide similar capabilities at far lower cost.

---

## The Rise of Mini Hedge Funds

A new model may emerge:

- One- to three-person hedge fund-like operations
- Individuals using AI to operate at an institutional level
- Platforms that support compliance and affiliation

Large institutions still retain advantages in:

- Computing power
- High-frequency infrastructure

But AI is making professional tools available to a much wider audience.

---

## Impact on Institutions

### Small and Mid-Sized Firms

These firms face major pressure:

- Their historical edge over retail investors is shrinking
- They must either:
  - Invest heavily in data, models, and infrastructure
  - Shift toward advisory or service roles

The middle ground is getting smaller.

### Large Institutions

Large firms continue to dominate through:

- High-frequency trading
- Advanced infrastructure
- Proprietary data

They are moving toward faster execution:

- Millisecond to microsecond to nanosecond speeds

---

## Workforce Transformation

AI will reshape financial jobs.

### Most at Risk

- Data entry
- Report writing
- Basic modeling
- Compliance checks

### The Emerging Role of Traders

- From execution to supervision of AI
- Toward strategy design and system oversight

A senior portfolio manager may now oversee work that once required an entire team.

---

## Who Will Not Be Replaced?

AI improves efficiency, but it does not create true alpha by itself.

The people least likely to be replaced are those who have:

- Strong frameworks and methods
- Strategic thinking and philosophical depth
- The ability to decide under uncertainty

AI cannot yet:

- Build original frameworks
- Understand regime shifts
- Navigate black swan events

Experienced investors still have advantages:

- Crisis experience
- Pattern recognition
- Macro intuition

AI is strong at:

- Pattern recognition in familiar environments
- Probabilistic optimization

But it struggles with:

- Structural breaks
- Geopolitical shocks
- Game-theoretic complexity

---

## A Bifurcated Future

The industry is likely to become more polarized:

- Top tier: strategy plus AI integration
- Bottom tier: low-skill, replaceable roles
- Middle layer: the most vulnerable

---

## Final Perspective: Efficiency vs Fairness

Technological progress usually favors efficiency, not equality.

Regulation should aim to:

- Prevent extreme distortions
- Ensure access to tools
- Avoid exploitation of loopholes

But complete fairness is not realistic.

---

## Historical Reminder

Past technological shifts have carried risks:

- The 1987 crash
- The LTCM collapse in 1998
- The quant crises of 2007 and 2008

But those risks are not a reason to stop progress.

The real challenge is:

- Managing risk while innovating
- Balancing efficiency and fairness

---

## Conclusion

AI trading agents are not just a tool upgrade. They represent:

- A restructuring of market participants
- A redistribution of capability
- A redefinition of competitive advantage

Those who adapt will thrive. Those who do not will be left behind.
