# The China Shock

Author: David Autor, David Dorn, and Gordon Hanson
Year: 2016
Source: [MIT Economics profile](https://economics.mit.edu/people/faculty/david-h-autor)

## Key Idea
The paper shows that large trade shocks can have persistent labor-market effects, including employment loss and regional adjustment costs. The broader lesson is that major technological or economic shocks can create long-lived disruption.

## Data
The paper uses U.S. local labor market data and trade exposure measures tied to import competition from China.

## Model / Method
The paper uses a local labor-market exposure design, relating import shocks to employment and wage outcomes across regions.

## Main Takeaway
Large shocks can produce persistent local labor-market damage rather than quick adjustment. For AI, that implies the important question is not only total gains but also transition speed, geography, and worker reallocation.

## Why It Matters For This Study
It helps frame how large-scale AI-driven disruption may produce geographic and distributional adjustment costs similar to other major shocks.
