# Tasks at Work: Comparative Advantage, Technology and Labor Demand

Author: Daron Acemoglu, Fredric Kong, and Pascual Restrepo
Year: 2024
Source: [MIT working paper PDF](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2025-03/Tasks%20at%20Work%20-%20Comparative%20Advantage%2C%20Technology%20and%20Labor%20Demand.pdf)

## Key Idea
The paper shows how task allocation across labor and capital explains recent labor-market trends. It distinguishes between technologies that augment labor, augment capital, automate tasks, and create new tasks, and argues that these channels have different implications for wages and inequality.

## Data
The paper draws on U.S. data and reduced-form evidence on labor demand, task displacement, wage changes, and sectoral shifts. It applies the framework to recent labor-market trends.

## Model / Method
The paper develops a task-based comparative-advantage model with multiple labor types, tasks, and technologies. It derives equilibrium wage equations and a propagation structure for ripple effects across sectors and occupations.

## Main Takeaway
The paper shows that technology can augment labor, augment capital, automate tasks, or create new tasks, and that these channels have different effects on wages and factor shares. The aggregate effect depends on the balance of those channels and on propagation across the economy.

## Why It Matters For This Study
This is a core framework for thinking about how agentic AI and automation can substitute for some tasks while complementing others.
